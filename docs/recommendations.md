# Operational recommendations

_Generated by `python -m sca.recommend` from the marts. Delivery numbers are **real Olist data (2016–2018)**. Anything labelled **[simulated]** comes from the synthetic inventory/PO layer and illustrates the method, not Olist sellers' actual stock._

**Baseline:** 99,435 orders · on-time delivery **93.2%** · cancel rate 1.2%.

## 1. Fix late deliveries in the weakest states (real)

| State | Orders | On-time % | Avg days late | Avg review |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| AL | 413 | 78.59 | 2.04 | 3.76 |
| MA | 747 | 82.57 | 1.83 | 3.76 |
| SE | 350 | 84.78 | 2.47 | 3.81 |
| PI | 495 | 86.13 | 1.85 | 3.92 |
| CE | 1,336 | 86.24 | 2.09 | 3.86 |

**Why it matters:** on-time orders average a **4.29** review; orders 4–7 days late average **2.1**. 6,381 orders (6.7%) arrived late.
**Action:** the five states above sit 9.6 points below the national 93.2%. Renegotiate carrier SLAs and widen the delivery estimate promised to these states — padding the estimate is the cheapest lever because on-time is measured against it.

## 4. Raise reorder points where the policy under-plans lead time **[simulated]**

The baseline policy plans with each supplier's *quoted* lead time. For the 400 product/DC series with any stockout weeks, I replayed the same demand and the same lead-time draws with the reorder point based on the supplier's observed **p90** lead time:

| Metric | Current policy | Revised policy |
|---|---:|---:|
| Reorder points raised | – | 121 of 400 series |
| Average fill rate | 88.2% | 90.4% |
| Share of weeks in stockout | 3.3% | 2.5% |
| Average inventory value | 126,439 | 137,568 (+8.8%) |

**Action:** apply the revised reorder points to the listed series, accept the extra inventory on unreliable-supplier SKUs, and re-run the simulation monthly with observed lead times.

**Context:** of 1,893 product/DC series, 53 are stocked out or at risk now, 494 are overstocked (>120 days), and 1,026 had no demand in the last 13 weeks — Olist demand is long-tail, so most SKU/DC pairs sell only occasionally. Slow movers are candidates for centralising in one DC rather than stocking in four.

## Caveats

- Delivery findings are from real 2016–2018 Brazilian marketplace data; they show the method, not today's performance.
- All inventory and PO numbers come from a seeded simulation (see `docs/assumptions.md`). The stockout rate and fill rate depend on the simulated policy and lead-time parameters, so treat impact estimates as directional.
- Seller IDs are truncated to their last six characters.
